## 레슨 1. Your First Day as an SME

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Your First Day as an SME |
| 짧은 이름 | First day |
| 부제 | What your role is, and how far you decide. |
| 노드 | Know the role · The day · Approval limits · Share with the lead |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about your first day after moving from agent to SME: what your role is, how you spend the day, and how far you decide. We look at it in four stages.
2. Until yesterday you were a senior agent on Sujin's team. From today you are the SME who backs up the same team of 12 people (Example). Your job is no longer handling your own contacts well, but keeping the team from getting stuck.
3. The first stage is knowing the role. The SME is the team's knowledge hub. You give answers on stuck contacts, approve or handle escalations, and help colleagues grow.
4. The SME does not evaluate or discipline agents. QA and the team leader do the evaluating. You are the one who approves and helps.
5. The second stage is knowing the day. Most of the day goes to escalation support, hard cases and mentoring agents. VOC write-ups and quality calibration meetings with QA come on top.
6. Requests come from two places. You walk the floor to help agents who raise a hand, and you answer questions that come in by messenger and the team chat.
7. The third stage is checking your approval limits. On day one, sit down with Sujin and list what you can approve and what goes to the team leader. For example, you approve compensation within set limits, and pass on requests outside policy and attendance or performance issues.
8. Not knowing the limits leads to two mistakes. You approve something beyond your authority, or you pass everything to the team leader and jam their day.
9. The fourth stage is sharing with the team leader. Agree early that you can take on attendance, coaching material and huddles when the lead is busy. Lesson 5 covers this in detail.
10. At the end of day one, jot down the questions you got. The ones that come up often are what you prepare first tomorrow.

## 레슨 2. Supporting Agents in Training or Just Trained

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Supporting Agents in Training or Just Trained |
| 짧은 이름 | New hires |
| 부제 | Don't wait for questions; go to them first. |
| 노드 | Learn ahead · Stay close · Check in first · Share with the lead |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about helping agents in training or just out of training settle into real contacts. This is when agents are most likely to leave.
2. Seojun finished training last week and joins the team today. He has the knowledge, but he has never been in front of a real customer.
3. The first stage is learning ahead. Ask the trainer what Seojun found hard in training, and think about which of today's contact types may be hard for him.
4. The second stage is staying close. For the first few days (Example), stay near Seojun's desk and go through his first few contacts with him. Listen in on calls, and read chats and emails with him before they go out.
5. Rather than giving the answer, show where to find it. When you point to the right knowledge base article, he can find it alone next time.
6. The third stage is checking in first. New hires hesitate to ask at all. Don't wait for a raised hand; go to him at set intervals (Example: once an hour) and ask.
7. Fix mistakes briefly on the spot, and say what he did well right away too. At this stage, one word of praise helps people hold on.
8. You stay at a different distance from an agent in OJT and one just out of training. During OJT you watch alongside the trainer; from nesting on, the SME stays closer.
9. The fourth stage is sharing with the team leader. At the end of the day, tell the team leader and the trainer briefly what Seojun did well and where he got stuck. The point is not to evaluate, but to decide where to help tomorrow.
10. If he keeps getting stuck in the same place, he may need more training. The team leader and the trainer decide; you gather the examples they decide on.

## 레슨 3. Supporting Existing Agents

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Supporting Existing Agents |
| 짧은 이름 | Escalations |
| 부제 | Approve, handle, or pass on what comes to you. |
| 노드 | Check the record · Decide · Report back · Share with the team |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about deciding quickly on contacts and escalations that existing agents pass to you: approve, handle yourself, or pass to the team leader.
2. Jimin has been an agent for two years. He knows the work, but today's contact is beyond his authority. He messages you to ask for approval.
3. The first stage is checking the record. See whether the ticket says what the customer wants, what Jimin already tried, and how the customer feels.
4. If the record is thin, ask Jimin briefly. That way the customer does not get the same questions again.
5. The second stage is deciding. Within your approval limits, you approve; if it needs you to speak to the customer, you take it over; if it is outside your limits, it goes to the team leader.
6. When you approve, note the reason too. When a similar request comes in, others can decide by the same standard.
7. When you take it over, first tell the customer you know what happened so far. Customers hate nothing more than being asked everything again by the next person.
8. The third stage is reporting back. When it is done, tell Jimin the outcome and the reason. Next time a similar contact comes, he can handle it himself.
9. The fourth stage is sharing with the team. When several agents ask the same question, sum it up once in the huddle or the team chat.
10. Experienced agents want answers fast. If deciding takes time, say first when you will answer, as in "I'll get back to you within 5 minutes" (Example).

## 레슨 4. Supporting Senior Agents Who Were Your Peers

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Supporting Senior Agents Who Were Your Peers |
| 짧은 이름 | Former peers |
| 부제 | Approve and help by the same standard, not by friendship. |
| 노드 | Reset the relationship · Same standard · Help with respect · Talk to the lead |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about approving and helping senior agents who were your peers by the same standard as everyone else, and moving to a relationship of mutual respect.
2. Junho joined on the same day as you, and your results were about the same. Now, when Junho needs an escalation approved, he has to ask you.
3. Both of you feel awkward. Junho may find it uncomfortable to get approval from a peer, and you may find it uncomfortable to judge a peer's request.
4. The first stage is resetting the relationship. In your first week, talk to Junho one on one. Say plainly what your role is and how he can best ask for help.
5. The second stage is the same standard. Judge Junho's requests by the same approval standard as other agents'. If you approve easily because you are close, other agents feel the standard depends on the person.
6. Being deliberately stricter with him is not fair either. Keep the standard the same; you can keep talking as casually as before.
7. The third stage is helping with respect. Senior agents know a lot. Rather than teaching, ask for Junho's judgment first and add only what is needed.
8. Junho's experience is a resource for the team. You can ask him to look at new hire Seojun's questions with you, or to review hard cases together.
9. The fourth stage is talking to the team leader. If Junho keeps skipping steps without approval, don't try to fix it alone; talk to the team leader. Attendance and performance belong to the team leader.
10. An honest talk in the first week is the starting point. In time, former peers can become the ones who back each other up best.

| a 피드백 | When it is looser for one person, other agents feel the approval standard depends on friendship. |

| 해설 | Apply the same standard, in a respectful way. |

| 해설 | Respect a senior's experience, but check the approval standard the same way for everyone. |
| 다음 레슨 연결 | The SME does not back up only agents. You also take on part of the team leader's work. |

## 레슨 5. Supporting the Team Leader

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Supporting the Team Leader |
| 짧은 이름 | Lead support |
| 부제 | Take on attendance, trend summaries and huddles. |
| 노드 | Attendance · Trend summary · Run the huddle · Report to the lead |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about taking on part of the team leader's work so the lead can spend time on coaching and running the team. There are three typical tasks.
2. Sujin leads a team of 15 people (Example), and today she is in client meetings all afternoon.
3. The first is attendance. When the team is large or the team leader is away, the SME checks attendance instead. You note who is not at their desk at start time, and any late or absence messages.
4. Covering means recording and reporting, nothing more. The team leader judges the reasons and decides on attendance action. If absences leave the team short, tell the team leader and the RTA right away.
5. The second is the scrubbing trend summary. From the tickets you scrub each day, collect what keeps showing up for each agent and sum it up briefly so the team leader can use it for coaching.
6. Write the summary with facts and examples. For example, "Jimin: skipped the same check step in 3 refund contacts this week," with the ticket numbers, is something the lead can coach from right away.
7. Write down behavior, not judgments about people. Not "careless," but "skipped the check step."
8. The third is running the huddle. When the client sends a policy or promotion update, the SME passes it to the team in the huddle.
9. Before the huddle, understand the update yourself and prepare how agents should explain it to customers. Questions raised in the huddle get answers posted back in the team chat.
10. Last, report to the team leader. Pass on attendance, the summary and the huddle briefly, so the lead sees at a glance what happened while away.

## 레슨 6. When Requests Pile Up at Once

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | When Requests Pile Up at Once |
| 짧은 이름 | Priorities |
| 부제 | Waiting customers first, and nobody left feeling dropped. |
| 노드 | Urgent first · Say when · Hand off · Call the lead |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about handling the most urgent requests first when they pile up on the floor and online, without anyone feeling dropped.
2. It is 11 in the morning (Example). Seojun has his hand up, Jimin's messenger is blinking, Junho has asked for approval, and two questions sit in the team chat.
3. The first stage is urgent first. The test is whether a customer is waiting right now. A customer on hold during a call, or in a live chat, comes first.
4. Contacts where the customer is not waiting live, such as emails and tickets, and general questions come next.
5. If two are equally urgent, take the one with an upset customer and the new hire's first. New hires tend to keep customers waiting longer.
6. The second stage is saying when. Tell anyone you can't reach right away when you will come, as in "I'll be there in 2 minutes" (Example). Knowing how long to wait is far better than no answer.
7. The third stage is handing off. Ask a senior agent like Junho to take general questions, or send just the knowledge base article first.
8. The fourth stage is calling the team leader. If requests keep piling up and customer waits get long, don't hold on alone; tell the team leader. The lead can help support or adjust staffing with the RTA.
9. Decide how you will keep up with both the floor and online. If center rules allow, carry a device for the messenger while walking the floor.
10. After the rush, look back at why it piled up. If the same question piled up, it becomes a huddle topic; if it piles up at the same time each day, change the support plan with the team leader.

## 레슨 7. Giving Knowledge Back to the Team

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Giving Knowledge Back to the Team |
| 짧은 이름 | Giving back |
| 부제 | Scrub every day, and give what you find back to the team. |
| 노드 | Daily scrubbing · VOC write-up · Calibration · Give it back |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about using daily scrubbing, VOC and calibration to see the team's trends, and giving what you learn back to the whole team.
2. Scrubbing is one of the SME's main jobs. You reread handled tickets and tickets that have piled up and find the real causes. You do a few every day (Example: 3 to 4 a day).
3. The first stage is scrubbing every day. Pick tickets in the order DSAT, CSAT, then unresolved, sum up each cause in a sentence or two, and sort it into the set categories. The procedure is in the Scrubbing SOP.
4. A little each day works because trends show only as they build up. Looking at many at once makes it easy to miss shared causes, and slows giving them back.
5. The second stage is the VOC write-up. Collect what customers keep saying: complaints, requests and views on the product. Causes agents cannot change become something to pass to the client.
6. The third stage is calibration. With QA and the team leader, each person scores the same contact and you align the differences. The SME brings what agents really face on the floor.
7. When calibration changes a standard, the SME is also the one who tells the agents first. No agent should be scored on a standard they did not know had changed.
8. The fourth stage is giving it back. Pass what you found to the team through huddles, the team chat and knowledge base updates.
9. If one person's mistake is the cause, send it in the summary for the team leader (lesson 5), not to the whole team. With the team, share only the lesson, without saying whose contact it was.
10. As the knowledge you give back builds up, the same questions drop, and so do the requests that come to you. That is how an SME grows a team.
