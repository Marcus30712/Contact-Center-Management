## 레슨 1. First Week: Leading Team Leaders

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | First Week: Leading Team Leaders |
| 짧은 이름 | First week |
| 부제 | Build new working relationships with the team leaders who were your peers until yesterday. |
| 노드 | Know the role · Split the work · Build relationships · Set reporting lines |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about what changes in your first week as an assistant manager, and how to build new relationships with the team leaders. The course follows Minseok through his first few months.
2. Minseok was a senior team leader in the Korean market, and this week he became the assistant manager over the Korean, Japanese and US markets. Sujin, his peer at the next desk until yesterday, now reports to him.
3. The first stage is knowing the role. A team leader leads agents, but an assistant manager leads team leaders and takes on part of the manager's work. You look at the whole project's numbers, not just your own team's.
4. The second stage is splitting the work. In his first 1:1 with Jeongeun, the manager, Minseok writes down what he owns and what she owns. Managing the market team leaders, preparing business review numbers, checking escalation reports and covering for the manager are his.
5. The third stage is building relationships. He sets a separate 1:1 with each team leader and first listens to how their team is doing and what they want from him. He leaves experienced team leaders to what they do well, and tells the new one he'll be around more often.
6. Things may feel awkward with Sujin after so many years together. Minseok is open about the new role, and makes clear that they'll still think problems through together, but decisions and reporting are now his.
7. The fourth stage is setting reporting lines. He agrees when, what and how the team leaders report to him, and what he passes up to Jeongeun. Clear reporting lines let him answer the client's questions quickly.
8. A word from Miso: In my first week after promotion, giving instructions to a former peer was the hardest part. It got easier the day I listened first and was honest about my new role.

## 레슨 2. Managing Team Leaders Across Markets

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Managing Team Leaders Across Markets |
| 짧은 이름 | Team leaders |
| 부제 | Use the weekly meeting and each market's metrics to tell which team leaders to trust and which to help. |
| 노드 | Weekly meeting · Compare metrics · Ask why · Decide where to help |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about looking at team leaders across markets by one standard, and telling where to trust and where to help.
2. Every Monday (Example), Minseok holds a weekly performance meeting with the three market team leaders. Sujin and Yuki are experienced team leaders, and Taeo became a team leader this month.
3. The first stage is the weekly meeting. Each team leader briefly covers last week's metrics, this week's plan and anything they need help with. Minseok doesn't rush to conclusions, and first listens to what each team is watching.
4. The second stage is comparing metrics. Contact types and client targets differ by market, so he doesn't rank raw numbers but reads each market against its own target. Answer rate, average handle time, quality score and attendance go into the same table.
5. The third stage is asking why. If the US market's quality score has dropped three weeks (Example) in a row, he shows Taeo the numbers and asks what has changed. Together they look at likely causes such as the share of new agents, policy changes and how often coaching happened.
6. The fourth stage is deciding where to help. Teams running well, like Sujin's and Yuki's, are left to it, and he only reviews the numbers with them. With Taeo, he builds a coaching plan together and checks the result at next week's meeting.
7. Treating all three team leaders the same isn't the same as being fair. Keep the standard the same, and match how much you help to each team's situation.
8. A word from Miso: As a team leader I only looked at my own team's numbers. Only as an AM did I learn that every market has a different target. Check the targets before you rank anyone.

## 레슨 3. Passing On and Confirming Instructions

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Passing On and Confirming Instructions |
| 짧은 이름 | Instructions |
| 부제 | Confirm that instructions from the client, the manager and the company reach every agent, and report back. |
| 노드 | Receive · Pass on · Confirm · Report |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about confirming that an instruction from above has reached every agent in every market, and reporting it to the manager.
2. On Friday afternoon, the client announces that its refund rules change next Monday (Example). Jeongeun, the manager, asks Minseok to pass it on to all three markets before Monday.
3. The first stage is receiving. Minseok sums up on one page what the instruction changes, from when and for whom. Anything unclear he checks with Jeongeun before passing it on.
4. The second stage is passing it on. He sends the same summary to all three market team leaders, and notes separately anything that differs by market. The team leaders pass it to agents through the huddle and the team chat.
5. The third stage is confirming. Sending it isn't the end. He gets a confirmation list from each team leader, and checks when agents who were off or on leave will hear it.
6. Taeo, the new team leader, only posted a notice in the team chat and didn't collect confirmations. Minseok shows him how to keep a confirmation list, and has him reach the two agents (Example) who missed it before their first shift.
7. The fourth stage is reporting. Minseok reports to Jeongeun the results by market, who hasn't heard it yet, and when they will. With this report, the manager tells the client the center is ready.
8. A word from Miso: I once reported that everything had been passed on just because notices went out, and wrong information went out on Monday morning. Since then I don't report until I have the lists.

## 레슨 4. Checking Escalation Reports

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Checking Escalation Reports |
| 짧은 이름 | Escalations |
| 부제 | Separate what the center can solve from what goes to the client, and check the team leaders' reports. |
| 노드 | How cases flow · Sort · Check · Keep records |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about knowing how escalations reach the client, and checking the reports team leaders send.
2. Escalations agents receive and customer complaints are gathered by the SME and reported to the team leader. The team leader reports them to the client by email, or at the interval agreed with the client, and handles them.
3. The first stage is knowing how cases flow. In the Korean market, Harin gathers the day's cases every evening (Example) and sends them to Sujin. Minseok receives all three markets' summaries.
4. The second stage is sorting. Cases the center can resolve itself are separated from cases that need the client's decision or action. What must go to the client, such as policy exceptions, system errors and personal data issues, follows what was agreed with the client.
5. The third stage is checking. Minseok checks that a team leader's report covers the customer details, what happened, what has been done so far and what is being asked of the client. If the same problem shows up in several markets, he has them reported together rather than one by one.
6. If a case that should go to the client is marked as solved in-house, he re-sorts it with the team leader before it goes out. A big case that might be reported late he also flags to Jeongeun right away.
7. The fourth stage is keeping records. Reported cases, the client's replies and the outcomes go into each market's record, ready to use as numbers and examples at the next business review.
8. A word from Miso: I fixed every team leader's report myself and became the bottleneck. Once I set four things every report must include, the team leaders filled them in on their own.

## 레슨 5. Preparing Business Review Materials and Numbers

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Preparing Business Review Materials and Numbers |
| 짧은 이름 | Review prep |
| 부제 | Build the WBR, MBR and QBR materials, check the numbers and hand them to the manager. |
| 노드 | Gather · Check · Tell the story · Hand over |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about preparing the materials and numbers the manager presents at business review meetings.
2. Business reviews are meetings the client attends, held weekly (WBR), monthly (MBR) and quarterly (QBR). Jeongeun presents, and the market team leaders in the room answer detailed questions about their markets.
3. The first stage is gathering. By three days (Example) before the meeting, Minseok collects each market's metrics, escalation records and headcount from the team leaders and the WFM team.
4. The second stage is checking. He checks that the numbers continue from the last report, use the same period and definitions, and match the system data. He finds that Yuki's table is off by a week, and recalculates it with her.
5. The third stage is telling the story. He doesn't just list numbers, but puts one point per page: what improved, what got worse, why, and the next step.
6. He writes down the questions the client is likely to ask, and has each market team leader ready to answer. He agrees with the team leaders in advance that questions they can't answer on the spot can be checked and sent after the meeting.
7. The fourth stage is handing over. By the day before the meeting, he gives Jeongeun the materials with a short note on which numbers changed and where questions are likely.
8. A word from Miso: I once had a manager quote a wrong number in front of the client. From that day, the first thing I check is whether the period and definitions match.

## 레슨 6. Gathering Information the Client Requests

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Gathering Information the Client Requests |
| 짧은 이름 | Client requests |
| 부제 | Collect from the team leaders, get the manager's sign-off and send it to the client. |
| 노드 | Pin down the request · Collect · Get sign-off · Send |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about the internal coordination of collecting what the client asks for from the team leaders, and sending it after the manager signs off.
2. On Tuesday morning, the client emails Jeongeun asking for last month's refund contact counts and typical cases by market by Thursday (Example). She hands the collection to Minseok.
3. The first stage is pinning down the request. He sets out what they want, for what period, in what format and by when. If the request is unclear, he doesn't guess but asks the client first through the manager.
4. The second stage is collecting. He sends all three market team leaders the same template and deadline, setting the deadline a day (Example) before the client's. That leaves time to review and fix.
5. Yuki sent the Japanese market's cases with customer names left in. Minseok asks her to remove the personal data and resend. Even in material for the client, no personal data beyond the agreed scope goes in.
6. The third stage is getting sign-off. He merges what he collected, reviews the numbers and cases, and reports to Jeongeun online for her sign-off. Even when the manager is away, he gets her sign-off by chat or email before sending.
7. The fourth stage is sending. He sends the signed-off material to the client through the agreed channel, and logs what was sent and when. If the client asks follow-up questions, he collects again the same way.
8. A word from Miso: I once sent something in a hurry without the manager's sign-off, and it clashed with a number she had already given the client. However urgent it is, always get that one sign-off.

## 레슨 7. Covering for the Manager

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Covering for the Manager |
| 짧은 이름 | Covering |
| 부제 | Sit in on client meetings, and answer after checking what you can't decide. |
| 노드 | Take over · Step in · Check, then answer · Hand back |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about filling in while the manager is away, and telling what you can decide from what you can't.
2. Jeongeun is on leave next week (Example). There's a weekly meeting with the client that week, and Minseok goes in her place.
3. The first stage is taking over. Before her leave, they write down the work in progress, what has been promised to the client, and what Minseok may decide versus what he must ask her. They also agree how to reach her if something urgent comes up.
4. The second stage is stepping in. Minseok reads the last meeting's notes and this week's numbers ahead of time, and goes into the meeting with the market team leaders. He runs it as usual, but doesn't guess at what the absent manager would say.
5. The third stage is checking, then answering. In the meeting, the client asks whether the center can add people next month. Headcount planning is the manager's call, so Minseok says he'll check with her and answer by a set date.
6. Right after the meeting, he sends Jeongeun a summary and the questions that need her decision. He takes her answers and passes them to the client on the promised day.
7. The fourth stage is handing back. When the manager returns, he hands over in one go what happened that week, any new promises to the client and the questions still open.
8. A word from Miso: The first time I covered, I felt I had to answer something in front of the client. That's when I learned that saying I'd check and get back actually builds trust.

## 레슨 8. Mentoring New Team Leaders and Getting Ready for What's Next

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Mentoring New Team Leaders and Getting Ready for What's Next |
| 짧은 이름 | Mentoring |
| 부제 | Teach what you've done, and learn by helping with the manager's work. |
| 노드 | Teach · Hand over · Help · Learn |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about passing on what you did as a senior team leader to new team leaders, and getting ready for the next role by helping with the manager's work.
2. Taeo has been a team leader for a month. Agent 1:1s, attendance and the weekly report all pile up at once, and he stays late every evening.
3. The first stage is teaching. Minseok goes through the weekly schedule and 1:1 note template he used as a senior team leader with Taeo, showing when to do what.
4. The second stage is handing over. After teaching, he lets Taeo do it himself and only reviews the results with him. Doing it for him makes that week easier, but Taeo gets stuck in the same place next week.
5. He sets a fixed 30-minute (Example) mentoring slot each week, and has Taeo bring one thing he's stuck on each time. He asks Sujin and Yuki, the experienced team leaders, to share cases with Taeo.
6. The third stage is helping. Minseok takes on bits of Jeongeun's work. He presents one page of the review materials himself, and sits in on headcount planning meetings to see how the numbers get decided.
7. The fourth stage is learning. In his 1:1 with the manager, they settle what he learned this month and what he'll take on next month. Whether he becomes a manager one day or moves elsewhere, this record is his preparation for the next role.
8. A word from Miso: I once did everything for a new team leader to help, and half a year later that team leader still couldn't stand alone. Teach, hand it over, and just review the results together.
