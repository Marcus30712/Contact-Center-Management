## 레슨 1. Your First Day as a Team Leader

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Your First Day as a Team Leader |
| 짧은 이름 | First day |
| 부제 | Before you run the team, decide what to learn about it and in what order. |
| 노드 | Know the people · Individual results · Team contribution · First plan |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about deciding, on your first day as a team leader, what to learn about the team and in what order before you start running it. The whole course follows this order.
2. From today you lead a team of 15 people (Example). The team doesn't know you, and you don't know how they have been working.
3. The first stage is getting to know the people. You need to know who is on the team, what skills each person has and what work they have done. The roster in lesson 2 is the starting point.
4. The second stage is individual results. Look at what numbers Jimin, Seojun and Junho have delivered and how they have actually worked. Lessons 3 and 4 cover this.
5. The third stage is the team's contribution. Look at what share this team has carried in the whole project, where it excelled and where it fell short. Lesson 5 covers this.
6. The fourth stage is the first plan. Decide how to help each team member, get the operating direction from your assistant manager, and plan for the whole team. Lessons 6 and 7 cover this.
7. You don't do this alone. The records and observations of Harin the SME, Dohyun from QA and Yuna the trainer, who have watched the team most closely, help a great deal.
8. Don't rush big changes in the first few weeks. Changing things before you understand them can shake what was working well.
9. On day one, greet the team briefly and tell them you will talk with each person over the next few weeks. They are also curious about what the new team leader will do.

## 레슨 2. Building the Roster

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Building the Roster |
| 짧은 이름 | Roster |
| 부제 | Gather in one place what each team member can do and has done. |
| 노드 | Basic details · Skills and work · Work pattern · Keep it current |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about gathering each team member's details on one roster, so you can see the whole team at a glance.
2. The first stage is basic details. Note each person's name, start date, the date they joined the team and their training class. Someone who joined recently like Seojun and someone in their second year like Jimin need different kinds of help.
3. The second stage is skills and work. Note the channels and contact types each person can handle and the work they have taken on. For example, Junho, as a senior agent, has been a new-hire buddy and has reviewed hard cases.
4. Ask people about what the records don't show. Harin the SME knows who gets stuck on which types, and Yuna the trainer knows who has had which training.
5. The third stage is the work pattern. Note shift times, scheduled leave and whether someone works part-time. You need these when planning staffing and coaching.
6. Put only facts on the roster. If you write impressions like "diligent," others who read it later have nothing to go on.
7. Keep personal details to what is needed, in the agreed place. Only the team leader and those who truly need it see the roster.
8. The fourth stage is keeping it current. The roster isn't a document you make once in the first week. Update it whenever someone learns a new skill or their work changes.

체크포인트:

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 1번 질문 | What fits the "Skills" column of the roster? |
| 보기 a (오답) | "Passionate and diligent; gets on well with everyone and is always first to help" |
| a 피드백 | An impression of personality doesn't tell you what work you can give someone. |
| 보기 b (정답) | "Chat and email; refund and delivery contacts; new-hire buddy experience" |
| 보기 c (오답) | "Third on the team for CSAT last month" |
| c 피드백 | Rankings belong in the results record. The skills column is for what someone can do. |
| 해설 | Write skills as the channels, contact types and work someone has taken on. |
| 2번 질문 | The records don't show it, but you want to know which types Seojun often gets stuck on. Whom should you ask? |
| 보기 a (오답) | Quietly ask the other agents on the team where Seojun seems to struggle lately. |
| a 피드백 | Asking colleagues to judge someone creates an uneasy mood in the team. |
| 보기 b (정답) | Ask Harin, the SME who has helped Seojun every day. |
| 보기 c (오답) | Don't ask; wait until it shows in the numbers. |
| c 피드백 | By the time it shows in the numbers, Seojun may have been stuck for a long time. |
| 해설 | The SME who helps agents every day knows best where they get stuck. |
| 다음 레슨 연결 | The roster shows who is on the team. Next, look at the results each person has delivered. |

## 레슨 3. Checking Individual Results

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Checking Individual Results |
| 짧은 이름 | Results |
| 부제 | Use the last few months of records to see how the numbers have moved. |
| 노드 | Gather records · Read the trend · Read it together · Make notes |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about gathering each team member's recent records and seeing how their numbers have moved. A few months of trend tell you more than one month's score.
2. The first stage is gathering records. Collect each person's ticket volume, average handle time (AHT), customer satisfaction (CSAT) and quality scores for the last 6 months (Example).
3. The second stage is reading the trend. See whether the numbers are steady, rising or falling. If Jimin's CSAT has been dropping for three months (Example), what happened when it started is a clue.
4. Don't look at one number alone. High volume with low quality may mean rushing, and long handling time with high CSAT may mean careful service.
5. The third stage is reading it together. Ask Dohyun from QA why the quality scores came out as they did and which items lose points most often. The person who gave the scores knows best why they are what they are.
6. From Harin the SME, get the per-agent trends she has seen in scrubbing. You can see who repeats the same mistakes and who is good at helping others.
7. The fourth stage is making notes. For each person, write a line or two on strengths, things to work on and questions to check. These notes are where lesson 6 begins.
8. Don't draw conclusions at this stage. Numbers only raise questions; you find the answers after seeing how people actually work.

체크포인트:

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 1번 질문 | Junho handles the most tickets on the team, but his quality score is below average. What do you do first? |
| 보기 a (오답) | Note him down as a top performer on volume alone. |
| a 피드백 | Looking at one number alone misses why quality is slipping. |
| 보기 b (정답) | Hear from QA which items lost points, and note questions to check in his recordings. |
| 보기 c (오답) | His quality score is low, so put him on the coaching list right away. |
| c 피드백 | Coaching without knowing why fixes the wrong thing. |
| 해설 | When numbers disagree, write questions to check rather than drawing conclusions. |
| 2번 질문 | What period fits for reviewing team members' results? |
| 보기 a (오답) | Last week only |
| a 피드백 | A single week can be good or bad by chance, so no trend shows. |
| 보기 b (정답) | The last few months (Example: 6 months) |
| 보기 c (오답) | Their whole time since joining (the longer, the more accurate) |
| c 피드백 | Mixing in old records blurs how they are doing now. |
| 해설 | Looking at the last few months shows both where people are now and how they are changing. |
| 다음 레슨 연결 | The numbers have raised questions. Now see for yourself how people actually work behind them. |

## 레슨 4. Listening to Calls and Reading Tickets

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Listening to Calls and Reading Tickets |
| 짧은 이름 | Look closer |
| 부제 | See how people really work behind the numbers. |
| 노드 | Pick contacts · Listen and read · Find habits · Check the cause |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about listening to call recordings and reading tickets yourself to see how each team member actually works.
2. The first stage is picking contacts. Pick a few per person (Example: 3 to 5), mixing ones that ended well with ones that ended in complaints. Start with contacts related to the questions in your lesson 3 notes.
3. The second stage is listening and reading. Follow the flow of greeting, understanding the problem, explaining and closing, and note what went well and where it wobbled.
4. Listening to Junho's calls, you hear him solve problems quickly but end without checking that the customer understood. That explains his high volume and low quality score.
5. The third stage is finding habits. Look for what repeats across several contacts, not a single mistake. Note good habits the same way.
6. It helps to listen to the same contacts with Dohyun from QA. Agreeing on what you each listen for keeps your standards aligned when you coach later.
7. The fourth stage is checking the cause. Ask Yuna the trainer whether a habit you found is the person's own or how they were taught in training.
8. If the training is the cause, the fix is the training, not coaching one person. Work it into the next training with the trainer.

체크포인트:

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 1번 질문 | In one of Jimin's calls, you notice she left out a minor explanation. How do you record it? |
| 보기 a (오답) | Note it as Jimin's habit, so you have grounds for coaching later. |
| a 피드백 | One contact isn't a habit yet. See whether it shows up in others. |
| 보기 b (정답) | One call could be chance, so listen to a few more and note it as a habit if it repeats. |
| 보기 c (오답) | To stop it repeating, call Jimin over on the spot, warn her and explain again. |
| c 피드백 | Pointing out a single contact while you're still getting to know the team makes people see the new team leader as a watchdog. |
| 해설 | Call it a habit when it repeats across several contacts. But if it is a required disclosure, correct it right away, even once. |
| 2번 질문 | Several agents are giving the same wrong explanation. What do you check first? |
| 보기 a (오답) | Call in each agent who got it wrong and coach them one by one. |
| a 피드백 | If the cause is the training, the next class will make the same mistake even after coaching. |
| 보기 b (정답) | Ask the trainer whether it was taught that way in training. |
| 보기 c (오답) | Point out the mistake at the team huddle. |
| c 피드백 | Pointing it out without knowing the cause makes agents feel they only did what they were taught. |
| 해설 | When several people make the same mistake, check the cause before the people. But if the wrong explanation is still reaching customers, share the correct one with the team right away. |
| 다음 레슨 연결 | You've seen how each person works. Now widen the view to what this team has contributed to the whole project. |

## 레슨 5. Understanding the Team's Contribution

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Understanding the Team's Contribution |
| 짧은 이름 | Contribution |
| 부제 | Sum up what this team has carried in the project, and its strengths and gaps. |
| 노드 | Goals and results · Compare teams · Strengths and gaps · One-page summary |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about summing up what this team has contributed to the whole project, where it excelled and where it fell short.
2. The first stage is goals and results. Put the goals the project set for this team (schedule adherence, quality, volume) next to the actual results of the last few months.
3. The second stage is comparing with other teams. Comparing with other teams on the same project shows whether a problem is just yours or the whole project's.
4. Comparing isn't for ranking. It's for finding what you can learn from how other teams do well.
5. The third stage is strengths and gaps. Write them specifically, for example "chat quality first in the project (Example), schedule adherence below target."
6. Look at the causes too. Check customer complaint topics in Harin's scrubbing and VOC records, and team-wide point losses in Dohyun's QA records.
7. Separate what the team can change from what it can't. If the cause is a policy or a system, it isn't the team's weakness but an issue to raise.
8. The fourth stage is the one-page summary. Put the team's goals, results, strengths, gaps and causes on one page. You take this summary to your assistant manager in lesson 7.

| 항목 | English |

| a 피드백 | A notice sent without knowing the cause only lets the same thing happen again. |

| 해설 | A summary should show results against goals, and why, at a glance. |

## 레슨 6. Deciding How to Work with Each Team Member

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Deciding How to Work with Each Team Member |
| 짧은 이름 | One by one |
| 부제 | Listen in a first one-on-one, and note how to help each person. |
| 노드 | First one-on-one · Listen · Compare · Personal notes |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about holding a short first one-on-one with each team member and combining it with what you've learned to decide how to help each person.
2. The first stage is the first one-on-one. Set aside about 15 minutes (Example) with each person. Say first that this is a chance to get to know each other, not an evaluation.
3. The second stage is listening. Ask about their strengths as they see them, what is hard, and what they would like to try. This is where you hear what the records can't tell you.
4. Jimin says that family matters have made night shifts hard lately. It lines up with when her CSAT started to drop. The reason behind the number comes from the person.
5. Hear personal matters only as far as needed, and don't share them without Jimin's permission. If her shifts need changing, follow the agreed process.
6. The third stage is comparing. Compare what they told you with the records and what you saw yourself. If they differ, don't decide which is right; keep watching.
7. The fourth stage is personal notes. For each person, write strengths, where to help, what to try giving them, and when to check again. For example, Junho steps back from the new-hire buddy role until his habit of checking before closing is in place, and you work on it with him.
8. The notes are a plan, not a label. Look at them again in a few weeks and fix what doesn't fit.

| a 피드백 | Opening a first meeting with an evaluation makes people defensive, and they won't say what they really think. |
| 보기 b (정답) | "This is for getting to know each other. I'd like to hear how work is going." |
| 보기 c (오답) | "If there's nothing in particular, we'll keep it short." |

## 레슨 7. Getting Direction from Your Assistant Manager or Operation Manager

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Getting Direction from Your Assistant Manager or Operation Manager |
| 짧은 이름 | Direction |
| 부제 | Take what you've learned, get the operating direction and make your first plan. |
| 노드 | Prepare the report · Hear the direction · First plan · Tell the team |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about reporting what you've learned to your assistant manager (AM) or operation manager (OM), getting the operating direction and making the team's first plan.
2. The first stage is preparing the report. From the team summary in lesson 5 and the personal notes in lesson 6, pick only what you must pass on. Leave team members' personal matters out of the report.
3. Your first meeting with Minseok, your assistant manager, is 30 minutes (Example). Briefly share how the team is now, why, and what you see as the priorities.
4. The second stage is hearing the direction. Confirm which metrics the project cares about most this quarter, what it expects from the team and what the team leader may decide alone.
5. If your priorities differ from the direction, give your view with evidence. If the direction is still set, follow it and keep a record of your view.
6. The third stage is the first plan. In line with the direction, decide what to do over the next month (Example). Write down whom to coach and how, what to change first across the team, and what to ask of the SME, QA and the trainer.
7. Start the plan small. Trying to change everything at once wears out both the team and you. Start with the one or two biggest problems.
8. The fourth stage is telling the team. At the team huddle, briefly share the direction ahead and the first goals. Don't single anyone out; just say where the team is heading together.
9. A month later (Example), meet Minseok again with the results. Understanding and planning, doing and checking keep turning in this order.
