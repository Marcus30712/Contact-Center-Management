## 레슨 1. SME

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | SME |
| 짧은 이름 | SME |
| 부제 | Become the person the team turns to when it's stuck. |
| 노드 | The work · Skills needed · Prepare now · What else they look for |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about knowing what an SME does, and what to prepare now to get there. The course follows Jimin, about a year after Go Live (Example), as she looks into her next role.
2. Jimin now handles most contacts on her own. Today, instead of asking Harin a question as she always did, she asks Harin about her work.
3. The first stage is the work. The SME is the team's knowledge hub. They help agents with contacts they're stuck on through chat or floor support, and stay close to new hires through OJT and nesting.
4. They gather frequent questions and the voice of the customer (VOC), find the causes, and report them to the team leader. When the team leader is away, they cover part of the team leader's work.
5. The second stage is the skills needed. Knowing the products and systems in depth is the baseline, plus the ability to analyze VOC and write it up as a report. Coaching peers and working through problems step by step matter too.
6. The third stage is preparing now. Keep a note of the contacts you often got stuck on, with their answers. It becomes material for helping peers who get stuck in the same place.
7. When the colleague next to you asks, don't just give the answer; show where to find it too. Helping someone who asks is the closest thing to an SME's work.
8. The fourth stage is what else they look for. When choosing an SME, they look at product knowledge and how much you've helped peers. It helps if the team leader and Harin already know you as "the one who explains things well."
9. A word from Miso: I only went around asking what I didn't know; I never thought to write down what I'd learned. If I'd kept notes from then on, I'd have had far more to show in my SME interview.

## 레슨 2. QA

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | QA |
| 짧은 이름 | QA |
| 부제 | Measure contact quality against the same standard. |
| 노드 | The work · Skills needed · Prepare now · What else they look for |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about knowing what a QA does, and what to prepare now to get there.
2. Every week in her score review, Jimin sees the quality scores Dohyun gave. She has always looked only at the score; today she asks about the work of the person who gives it.
3. The first stage is the work. QA checks call recordings, chats and emails against a set scoring standard and gives scores. They send the results back to the team leader and agents so coaching has evidence to stand on.
4. QA also runs calibration, where QA, the team leader, the SME and the trainer listen to the same recording and line up their scores. If scores differ by who gives them, agents stop trusting them.
5. The second stage is the skills needed. You need to know the standard exactly and score carefully enough that anyone would reach the same score. Basic data analysis, to see where points are often lost, matters too.
6. The third stage is preparing now. When you get your score sheet, read why points were lost on each item, not just the total. The better you know the standard, the better your own contacts get and the more you see like a QA.
7. If you can play back your recording, or when you listen to it together in a score review, score it yourself with the sheet and compare with Dohyun's score. The items that differ are the standards you don't know yet.
8. The fourth stage is what else they look for. When choosing a QA, they look at whether your own quality scores are steadily high and whether you understand the standard exactly. They also look for the fairness to hold a close friend's contacts to the same standard.
9. A word from Miso: Every time I got a score sheet, I looked at the score and put it away. If I'd really read the standard, I'd have repeated fewer mistakes, and QA would have caught my eye sooner.

## 레슨 3. Trainer

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Trainer |
| 짧은 이름 | Trainer |
| 부제 | Help newcomers learn it right. |
| 노드 | The work · Skills needed · Prepare now · What else they look for |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about knowing what a trainer does, and what to prepare now to get there.
2. Yuna was the person Jimin met in her first week of training. Back then she was busy taking notes; now she's curious about the work of the person at the front.
3. The first stage is the work. The trainer runs new-hire training, teaches the products, services, systems and policies, and checks learning with tests. They decide with the team leader who graduates.
4. When a new product or policy comes out, they teach agents who are already working too. They also put the mistakes QA often finds into the training material.
5. The second stage is the skills needed. You need to explain hard things in plain words and to speak in front of a group. Basic data analysis, to read from test results where trainees get stuck, matters too.
6. The third stage is preparing now. Steadily high training and test scores are the first step. You can't teach what you don't know.
7. When new hires join, explain in plain words the parts that confused you at first. When there's a chance to explain a policy change briefly at a team meeting, raise your hand.
8. The fourth stage is what else they look for. When choosing a trainer, they look at your recent training and test scores and whether you can explain clearly in front of people. Some centers ask for a short demo lesson (Example: 5 minutes) in the interview.
9. A word from Miso: In training, when I didn't understand something, I just memorized it. If I'd kept asking why until I could explain it in my own words, teaching someone later would have been much easier.

## 레슨 4. Team Leader

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Team Leader |
| 짧은 이름 | Team leader |
| 부제 | Lead the whole team to its goals. |
| 노드 | The work · Skills needed · Prepare now · What else they look for |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about knowing what a team leader does, and the path from agent to team leader.
2. Jimin has a score review with Sujin every week. Today, after the review, she asks what path Sujin took to become a team leader.
3. The first stage is the work. A team leader manages the results of 10 to 15 team members (Example) and leads the team to its goals through coaching and feedback. Looking after schedules and attendance, and hearing team members' concerns, are part of it too.
4. They check the team's metrics every day, hold score reviews every week and performance reviews every month. Upward, they report the team's results and problems to the assistant manager and the operation manager.
5. The second stage is the skills needed. You need to read performance data and to manage people. Above all, you need the leadership and feedback that make team members want to try again.
6. The third stage is preparing now. It varies by center, but rather than going straight from agent to team leader, many work as an SME and serve as team leader backup first.
7. As an agent, start by practicing reading your own metrics. In your score review, before Sujin speaks, say first what went well this week and what to fix.
8. The fourth stage is what else they look for. When choosing a team leader, they look at experience leading people as an SME or team leader backup, and completed leadership training. They also look at whether the team already trusts and follows you.
9. A word from Miso: I thought leadership would just appear once I became a team leader. The people who listened to their peers and looked for answers with them as agents were the ones who became good team leaders.

## 레슨 5. Getting Ready for the Next Role

| 항목 | English |
|---|---|
| 제목 | Getting Ready for the Next Role |
| 짧은 이름 | Ready |
| 부제 | Every role looks at the same four things. |
| 노드 | Attendance · Clean record · Quality and test scores · Team contribution |

스텝:

1. This lesson is about knowing the four things every role looks at, and building them from today. Selection criteria differ by center, but the basics are similar.
2. Jimin saw a posting for an SME opening next month. Not sure whether she should apply, she asked Miso.
3. The first stage is attendance. Above all, attendance matters. Roles above agent work with many agents and other departments, so when a seat is often empty, the flow breaks when the team or project needs it.
4. However high your quality and contribution, low attendance makes it hard to be chosen. When you're sick, rest. But cutting the gaps you can avoid, like lateness and no-shows, builds the most trust.
5. The second stage is a clean record. People with no complaints from customers or the client in the last 6 months (Example) are preferred. They also check for any disciplinary record for breaking company rules.
6. Records fall outside the window as time passes. If you have a time in mind to apply, your preparation starts 6 months (Example) before it.
7. The third stage is quality and test scores. You need to know the current project's products, services and systems better than the other applicants. The objective measures are QA quality scores and the trainer's training and test scores from the last 3 to 6 months (Example).
8. Steady scores over several months beat one high month. The habit of picking one thing to fix in each weekly score review shows up here as numbers.
9. The fourth stage is team contribution. Having helped the agents around you is something extra you can show in the interview.
10. It's even better if you've found why customers often complain in VOC and proposed fixes logically, using a method like root cause analysis (RCA). Keep a record so you can say in the interview when, what, why, and what came of it.
11. A word from Miso: I only started preparing after the posting went up. Attendance, record, scores and contribution are all the sum of the past few months, so the day the posting goes up is already late.
